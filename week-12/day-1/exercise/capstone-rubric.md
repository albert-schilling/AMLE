# Capstone Rubric

This rubric is used **twice**: today, to check your Day 1 project brief in the pair review; and across the
whole capstone (Weeks 12–14), as the bar your finished project is graded against. Read it now so you know
what "good" means from day one — the strongest projects are the ones that keep this rubric in view the
whole way.

Each row is scored **1–4**: **1** missing/unclear · **2** partial · **3** solid · **4** exemplary. Aim
for **3 or better on every row**. (This is the same 1–4 scale you will use for LLM-as-judge quality
scoring in the evaluation plan — get used to it here.)

---

## Part A — Discovery (Week 12): scored on today's brief

| # | Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | **Problem frame** | no clear frame | some slots vague | all four slots filled and specific | frame is sharp; you could build straight from it |
| A2 | **User is specific** | "everyone" | a broad group | one named role/persona | one person in one concrete situation |
| A3 | **Capability is one job** | many jobs bundled | two jobs | one clear capability | one capability, obviously the most valuable one |
| A4 | **Archetype fit** | none chosen | chosen but ill-fitting | fits, justified in a line | fits, justified, runner-up considered, simplest-that-works |
| A5 | **Scope in/out** | no scope | in-scope only | in **and** out, ≥ 3 out-of-scope items | scope is a credible solo three-week v1 |
| A6 | **Metrics have targets** | no metrics | metrics without numbers | 2–3 metrics with target numbers | targets are outcome **and** quality, and realistic |
| A7 | **Risks named** | none | one vague risk | top risks named | risks named with a de-risking plan |

**Today's minimum bar:** A1, A5, and A6 at **3+**. Those three — a fillable frame, a real scope, and
checkable targets — are what the rest of the week builds on directly.

---

## Part B — Execution (Weeks 13–14): scored on the finished project

Not assessed today — shown so you know where the brief is heading. Your Day 1 choices should make these
achievable.

| # | Criterion | What "3+" looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | **Works end-to-end** | the in-scope happy path runs start to finish on a real input |
| B2 | **Evaluated** | runs against the golden set; the Day-1 metrics are actually measured (Tue/W13) |
| B3 | **Grounded & correct** | outputs are correct and cite/ground their claims where relevant |
| B4 | **Sound architecture** | the topology matches the archetype; components are separable and testable |
| B5 | **Observability** | runs are traced; you can show what the agent did and why (W8) |
| B6 | **Robustness & safety** | handles the obvious failure cases; basic defense-in-depth (W11/W14) |
| B7 | **Demo & docs** | a clear demo and a README a stranger could follow (W14) |

---

## How coaches use this

- **Circulate with Part A open.** Ten seconds per learner: glance at A1, A5, A6.
- **Steer, don't rewrite.** Ask the question that exposes the gap ("who exactly? what number?"); let the
  learner fix their own idea.
- **The two rows that catch the most problems** are **A2/A3** (vague user or multi-job capability) and
  **A6** (metrics with no number). Fix those two and most briefs jump a level.
