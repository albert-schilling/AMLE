## How to use this rubric

Choose the highest level fully supported by current evidence. Cite specific evidence package
IDs or links. Do not rate intended work, effort, or an untested claim. A lower honest rating
with a clear next step is stronger than an unsupported high rating.

### Rating scale

- **1 — Not yet evidenced:** missing, inaccessible, or only asserted.
- **2 — Partially evidenced:** some behavior works, but gaps or inconsistent evidence remain.
- **3 — Demonstrated:** target behavior is supported under stated conditions.
- **4 — Strongly evidenced:** demonstrated across meaningful conditions with reproducibility,
  trade-offs, and limitations made explicit.

## Criterion prompts

### B1 — Works end-to-end

- Does the in-scope happy path run start to finish on a real input?
- Are commit, environment, configuration, and dependencies identified?
- Is fallback evidence current and honestly labelled?

### B2 — Evaluated

- Does the project run against the golden set?
- Are the Day 1 metrics actually measured?
- Are suite, versions, thresholds, row-level outcomes, regressions, and uncertainty recorded?

### B3 — Grounded & correct

- Are outputs correct for the evidenced cases?
- Are claims cited or grounded where relevant?
- Are unsupported, conflicting, or missing-source cases handled honestly?

### B4 — Sound architecture

- Does the topology fit the selected archetype and actual problem?
- Can a reviewer follow data, context, state, model, and tool flow?
- Are components separable, testable, and justified by constraints and trade-offs?

### B5 — Observability

- Can a reviewer inspect a representative end-to-end run?
- Does the evidence show what the agent did and why?
- Are identifiers, redaction, cost/latency/error signals, and remaining blind spots addressed?

### B6 — Robustness & safety

- Are project-specific risks and obvious failure cases named and tested?
- Are layered controls, escalation, residual risks, privacy boundaries, and regressions visible?
- Is “safe” avoided as a universal claim?

### B7 — Demo & docs

- Does the README let a stranger set up, run, and verify the project?
- Does the demo connect user need, architecture, working path, evidence, and limitations?
- Are fallback artifacts current, labelled, and easy to navigate?

## Pace-aware completion

### Minimum

- [ ] Every B1–B7 criterion has a rating and evidence citation or explicit “not yet evidenced.”
- [ ] The working path, one trace, and one evaluation artifact are cited.
- [ ] Limitations and next steps are explicit.

### Target

- [ ] B2, B5, and B6 cite inspectable evaluation, observability, and safety evidence.
- [ ] Ratings distinguish demonstrated conditions from broader unknowns.
- [ ] Gaps to the next level are testable.

### Stretch

- [ ] Ratings include reproducibility, uncertainty, cost/latency, or operational trade-offs.
- [ ] Audience feedback is incorporated into one or more rating explanations.
- [ ] A reviewer can navigate from every material claim to an artifact.
